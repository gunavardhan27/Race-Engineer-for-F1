# General Car Handling & Setup Notes

## Brake Bias
- Shifting brake bias forward (higher percentage): improves straight-line braking stability, reduces rear step-out, but increases front tyre locking and entry understeer.
- Shifting brake bias rearward (lower percentage): encourages car rotation on corner entry and during trail-braking, but increases risk of sudden rear snap or rear wheel lockup.

## Differential (On-Throttle)
- Higher lock percentage (more locked): provides better traction in a straight line out of slow corners, but causes on-throttle understeer as rear wheels resist turning at different speeds.
- Lower lock percentage (more open): allows the inside rear wheel to rotate freely, reducing understeer on exit and aiding rotation, but increases inside wheel spin under aggressive throttle application.

## Differential (Off-Throttle)
- Higher lock: increases stability during corner entry under trail braking, stabilizes the rear end.
- Lower lock: improves turn-in responsiveness into slow and medium-speed corners.

## Front Wing Angle
- Adjustable only during pit stops in clicks or steps.
- Adding front wing (more clicks): adds front downforce, shifts aerodynamic balance forward, reducing medium and high-speed understeer, but increases rear instability.
- Removing front wing (fewer clicks): calms high-speed rear instability, reduces drag, but promotes high-speed understeer.

## ERS Deployment Modes
- Hotlap / Overtake: maximum electrical battery deployment for attack and defense.
- Medium: balanced race deployment matching standard lap energy budget.
- None: cuts deployment entirely to rapidly recharge battery or save energy.

## Driving Line Adjustments
- Understeer cure: brake slightly earlier, trail brake deeper to keep load on front axle, delay throttle application until car is pointed.
- Oversteer cure: smooth out throttle application on exit, avoid aggressive kerb climbing, shift brake bias one click forward.
